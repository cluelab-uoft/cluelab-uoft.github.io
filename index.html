Computational Linguistics for Understanding & Explanation

Finding the clues language leaves in the mind, and in society.

The CLUE Lab studies how people comprehend language — and how that comprehension can be modeled, measured, and put to use — by combining computational tools with the cognitive science of language.

About

Our mission

CLUE Lab takes a theory-driven, experimental approach to language science — integrating computational linguistics with the cognitive science of language to understand how humans use, process, and are affected by language, and to explain what our models reveal.

Shohini Bhattasali, PhD
Principal Investigator · Assistant Professor, Department of Language Studies & Department of Linguistics, University of Toronto Scarborough

Dr. Bhattasali received her PhD in Linguistics from Cornell University (2019, graduate minor in Cognitive Science), advised by John Hale. She was subsequently a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Maryland's Department of Linguistics and Institute for Advanced Computer Studies, working primarily with Philip Resnik.

Her research sits at the intersection of computational linguistics and the cognitive science of language, and has been supported by grants including a UofT Data Sciences Institute Catalyst Grant.

Research

Two lines of inquiry, one question

How do humans use and understand language — and how can computational tools help us model, and improve, that process?

Computational Neurolinguistics

We use contemporary computational models to gain insight into language comprehension in the brain — sitting at the interface of natural language processing, psycholinguistics, and cognitive neuroscience. Current questions include how syntactic and semantic constraints shape top-down prediction during sentence processing, and how linguistic processing interacts with attention, memory, and cognitive control.

Computational Social Science

We examine how linguistic patterns and features in real-world datasets can help uncover bias and misinformation, using NLP tools such as topic models and language models on text from domains like social media and public health repositories — aiming to help improve these systems.
